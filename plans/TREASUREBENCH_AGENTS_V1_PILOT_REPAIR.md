# TreasureBench Agents v1 pilot repair

This living ExecPlan governs one bounded, non-inferential DD-010 engineering
adjudication of the provider and protocol instrument failures in the fixed
TreasureBench Agents v1 sealed pilot. It has two inseparable diagnostic tracks:
(1) provider-error and missingness adjudication and (2) action-budget
contract-conformance adjudication. The original pilot remains immutable and
quarantined as `sealed-pilot-quarantined-provider-failure`. Phase A is
public/offline only. Phase B may begin only after the owner creates the exact
interactive read-only diagnostic authorization and resumes the same Codex
session.

## Purpose

Determine the exact attempt, retry, run, protocol, missingness, and downstream
effects of the two retained Anthropic provider errors; independently reproduce
aggregate final-action cardinality conformance across all 500 retained runs;
repair the public instrument contract before any future execution; establish a
prospective failure policy; and require a separately registered fresh sealed
pilot. This plan creates no scientific result.

## Instrument repair

The public contract audit establishes a prospective action-budget defect:
registered architectures grant one final action per agent, while the schema,
parser, evaluator, and Method B did not enforce that cardinality. Phase A must
repair the final schema and prompt contract, parser, orchestration,
conservative invalid-output scoring, Method B reconstruction, protocol
compliance, invalid-action accounting, and metric range invariants. Non-final
multiple-action lists remain permitted only as explicitly labeled proposals;
they cannot be confused with or credited as final actions. Method C must reject
extra or missing final records/actions, invalid vocabulary, architecture
rights, message/round violations, source-choice violations, action-capacity
mismatch, and out-of-range metrics before performance interpretation.
Provider-specific repair remains evidence-dependent on Phase B.

## Discoveries and surprises

- The existing pilot synthetic audit is deliberately bound to the historical
  sealed-pilot branch and refuses from main or this repair branch. The new
  diagnostic audit therefore needs its own exact branch/commit contract; the
  refusal is not evidence of pilot corruption.
- The runtime ledgers preserve provider attempts separately from orchestration
  protocol errors. A recovered transport attempt can therefore remain in the
  operational record without creating a terminal run failure.
- The retained adapter record stores normalized error class and safe
  operational metadata, not an unrestricted raw provider error body. Phase B
  may prove an exact cause, a bounded class, or evidence insufficiency; the
  tool must not manufacture missing detail.
- Current provider contracts are relevant to prospective policy but cannot
  retroactively change the frozen 2026-07-24 execution decision.
- The historical synthetic pilot authorization incorrectly required the old
  live-execution branch, which made its offline rehearsal and corruption suite
  fail on every descendant work branch. The check now remains exact for real
  authorization while synthetic-only rehearsal is portable. Existing tests
  still prove synthetic authorization cannot authorize live execution.
- Paper validation regenerates two provenance copies with the current build
  commit. Because paper sources and provenance are out of scope, those
  mechanical build-only changes were restored byte-for-byte after the exact
  seven PDF hashes and 119-page count passed.
- The action-rights source declares exactly one final action per agent, while
  the registered JSON Schema allowed one through six actions and the live
  provider schema set no item-count bound. The parser enforced nonempty,
  unique, in-vocabulary arrays but not final cardinality. Both metric methods
  flattened those arrays, so exact A/B agreement could preserve the same
  semantic defect and coverage could escape its registered proportion range.
- The aggregate defect is not rare enough to treat as an isolated provider
  event: 137 of 500 runs violate final cardinality under the authoritative
  contract. The family, architecture, model, provider, and round
  reconstructions were completed privately but are withheld because
  publishing them could expose comparative or task-level structure.
- The minimum retained record for the terminal schema-or-parameter event
  proves terminality but does not preserve enough normalized locus detail to
  distinguish an invalid request, exact-route capability rejection,
  provider-side schema rejection, or returned-output nonconformance.
  Evidence insufficiency is the final diagnosis, not a placeholder for a
  guessed cause.
