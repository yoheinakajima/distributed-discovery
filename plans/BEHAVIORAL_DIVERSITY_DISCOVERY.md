## Scope

One task branch and draft PR hold one coordinated study. Internal research
roles may work in parallel, while the coordinator serializes shared-source,
registry, and ledger changes. Candidate mechanisms are prospective slots,
not frozen models or promised findings:

| Slot | Candidate mechanism | Admission question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| track-01 | Persistence and role rotation | Does stable specialization add value beyond rotating assignments or homogeneous intermediate behavior under a fair information budget? |
| track-02 | Adaptation under nonstationarity | Can heterogeneous responses outperform adaptive generalists when the environment changes, without building the advantage into the model? |
| track-03 | Commitment and switching costs | Does preserving paths or paying to change them alter the value of a mixture relative to well-specified dynamic alternatives? |
| track-04 | Communication timing | Does the timing of sharing affect search diversity and implementation independently of the amount of evidence or action capacity? |
| track-05 | Repeated information externalities and attribution | Do learning spillovers or attribution in repeated search reveal a distinct, falsifiable residual question beyond established experimentation and information-sharing models? |
| track-06 | Reliable execution, common failures, and downside | How do correlated execution failure, losses, or downside constraints change diversification benefits beyond standard reliability and portfolio results? |

Each slot may narrow, pivot within the authorized mechanism scope, defer, or
stop after its saved novelty audit. No seventh slot or replacement primary
run is silently added. A stopped or null track counts as a legitimate
research outcome.

## Assumptions

The academic report is a landscape search rather than a systematic-review
certificate. Novelty remains provisional even after a search. Synthetic
policies are computational constructions, not measured human types. The
environment's objective, information, timing, failure law, and admissible
actions must be explicit before interpreting an advantage.

Comparators should include homogeneous intermediate behavior, adaptive
generalists, rotating specialists, fixed mixtures, and a coordination upper
benchmark when mathematically meaningful. Each admitted comparison must
declare equal evidence and action budgets or explicitly report the difference.
An upper benchmark may have greater coordination authority, but that
advantage must be labeled and cannot count as an equally decentralized policy.
